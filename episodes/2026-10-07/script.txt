Hi, and welcome to Small Models, Real Systems, the weekly research digest by Mylari. This is edition two, for Wednesday the seventh of October, 2026. Each week we pick new work on fine-tuning small models for metadata generation and content understanding. This week: five picks, one theme. The large model moves offline, as a labeler, teacher or judge, and a small student does the serving.

First, Walmart. In a paper posted on the third of October, the search team improves document expansion: generating queries a shopper might type, and appending them to each product before indexing. The model is a T5-base, just two hundred and twenty million parameters. After supervised fine-tuning, they score its own outputs with an in-house relevance classifier, pair exact-match queries against irrelevant ones, and train with Direct Preference Optimization, or DPO. That roughly halved irrelevant generations, while the count of new tokens held steady. A relevance filter brings the reduction to sixty-four percent. A second DPO round just overfit. In a two-week A/B test, search add-to-cart rate rose zero point three six percent, and it now serves full traffic.

Second, a recruiting system from BroutonLab and Curately, accepted at EMNLP and posted on the second. Instead of one opaque match score, it predicts eight named dimensions, like seniority and experience recency. A GPT-5.2 mini labeler, its prompts refined from recruiter feedback, now runs offline only. Its labels train a Qwen3 four-billion cross-encoder, whose soft outputs are distilled into Qwen3 Embedding, six hundred million parameters, with LoRA and small per-dimension heads. With cached embeddings, it scores about fifty-five hundred resumes per second on one CPU. Fidelity to the labeler is a macro F1 of zero point eight five, and it matches almost ninety-six percent of recruiter-recorded decisions, a non-blinded check. Seniority is the weak spot.

Third, multilingual entity extraction for privacy redaction, from Jonathan Graehl at RWS Language Weaver, posted on the twenty-ninth of September and revised yesterday. Frontier-model annotations in thirty-five languages train XLM-R large, an encoder, with a simple span-tagging head. Two tricks matter. Human-labeled corpora from other schemas are replayed, but types they never annotated are masked rather than treated as negatives, worth ten points of human-gold F1 in a precursor experiment. And a learned one-character boundary fix repairs subword errors. Redaction F1 reaches eighty-eight point eight, against sixty-nine point one for GLiNER2 and fifty-seven point three for Microsoft Presidio. A full training run costs about five dollars of GPU time.

Fourth, moderation in production. An Amazon Web Services post from the first of October covers uniopen, a Taiwanese retail membership platform. Each conversation window gets two labels: one of nine behaviors, and its subject: brand, other, or forbidden. LoRA fine-tuning on about thirty-four hundred windows took Amazon Nova 2 Lite, whose size isn't disclosed, from zero point five nine to zero point eight four behavior macro F1, and from zero point four two to zero point eight three on subject. Then a free win: switching the output from JSON to a simple line format pushed both past their production targets. Both metrics are release gates, and only human-verified corrections enter training.

Finally, an evaluation lesson, slightly older, from the twenty-fourth of September. Billennium fine-tuned HerBERT, a hundred-and-twenty-four-million-parameter Polish encoder, into a five-category safety classifier, and compared it with Bielik Guard. The test benchmark is ninety-seven percent crime-positive, so always predicting crime scores zero point nine one micro F1. Their model's zero point nine three barely clears that. An apparent macro F1 win vanished once both systems got the same threshold tuning. The out-of-distribution gap was calibration, not ranking, and per-category temperature scaling recovered it.

So, the patterns this week. The big model becomes an offline labeler, teacher or relevance judge. The student gets genuinely small: an encoder, a T5-base, a six-hundred-million-parameter embedder. Feedback loops keep improving the labels. And honest evaluation needs degenerate baselines and matched protocols. Links and numbers are in the companion post. Thanks for listening, and see you next week.
